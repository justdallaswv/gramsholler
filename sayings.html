Mountain Wit & Wisdom

Gram-isms

"Old sayings are like old paths—they wouldn't be there if they didn't lead somewhere sensible." Down in the holler, these bits of folk wisdom have guided our family for generations.

"Ain’t got the sense God gave a goose"

Acting entirely foolishly. Geese aren't famous for brilliant choices, so having less sense than one means you're in a bit of trouble.

"Cold as a well digger's ass"

Bitterly cold weather. Well diggers spend all day deep underground in damp, sunless dirt holes, away from any heat.

"Crazy is a shit house rat"

Behaving completely wild, erratic, or totally unhinged.

"Cute as a bugs ear"

Unbelievably adorable, tiny, or precious.

"Dumb as a doorknob"

Completely oblivious, unresponsive, or lacking intelligence.

"Dumber than a box of rocks"

Heavy-headed and slow-witted. Stones are silent, solid, and entirely empty of thoughts.

"Fine as a frog hair"

Feeling fantastic or in excellent health. Since frogs don't have hair, something split as thin as a frog's hair must be extraordinary.

"Fit as a fiddle"

In excellent physical shape, healthy, and full of energy.

"He's cutting a shine"

Showing off, dancing wildly, or acting up to be the center of attention.

"He wouldn’t hit a lick at a snake"

Incredibly lazy. Even if a venomous snake was directly in front of them, they wouldn't lift a hand to defend themselves.

"Hinton’s up the river"

A regional phrase meaning someone is out of pocket or sent away, referencing the town of Hinton up the New and Greenbrier rivers.

"If the good Lord’s willing and the creek don’t rise"

My plans are set, barring any natural disasters. In the hills, a flash flood can rapidly wash out low-water paths and isolate you at home.

"It’s a gullywasher out there"

A massive, heavy rainstorm. The kind of downpour that carves deep ditches (gullies) straight into the hillsides.

"It’s pouring the rain"

Classic regional phrasing for a severe downpour, adding emphasis by treating "the rain" as a singular, grand event.

"Madder than an old wet hen"

Absolutely furious. Dunking a stubborn, nesting hen in cold water is an old farming trick to break her mood, leaving her entirely outraged.

"Nutty as a fruitcake"

Strange, wacky, or highly eccentric. Packed completely full of literal nuts, just like a holiday dessert.

"Old as methuselah"

Incredibly old. References the biblical figure Methuselah, who was said to have lived for nearly a thousand years.

"Pretty as a speckled pup"

Charming, unique, and highly endearing. A speckled hound puppy is a classic, cheerful mountain sight.

"Raining cats and dogs"

An old-fashioned phrase for an exceptionally heavy storm.

"Slow as molasses"

Taking a long time to finish a task. Home-cooked sorghum molasses is extremely thick and takes its sweet time pouring.

"Sweating like a whore in church"

Incredibly nervous, anxious, or sweating profusely because you are somewhere you feel entirely out of place.

"You ain’t just whistlin’ Dixie"

You are absolutely correct, and what you just said carries serious weight. Not just making empty conversation.

"You’re damn skippy"

An emphatic, enthusiastic sign of agreement. Meaning "You better believe it" or "You're exactly right."
